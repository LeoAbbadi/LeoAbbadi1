Prologue · Sparta · Before the games

The Ghost of Sparta

Origin Who is Kratos?

Kratos was a brutal Spartan captain who couldn't stand losing. When a barbarian army was about to kill him, he promised to serve Ares, the God of War, in exchange for victory. Ares gave him the Blades of Chaos, chained to his arms forever.

But Ares's trick made him kill his own wife and daughter. Their ashes stuck to his skin and turned it white. From that day on, people called him the Ghost of Sparta.

Chapter I · Greece · 2005

God of War

Objective Kill Ares, the God of War ✓ Completed

For ten years Kratos served the gods, hoping to forget his nightmares. Athena promised to forgive his sins if he managed to destroy Ares, who was attacking Athens. To kill a god, Kratos needed Pandora's Box, hidden in a temple on the back of the Titan Cronos. With its power, he defeated Ares.

But the nightmares stayed, and Kratos would rather die than live with them. Athena saved him, and the gods let him take Ares's place as the new God of War.

Chapter II · Greece · 2007

God of War II

Objective Change your fate ✓ Completed

As the new God of War, Kratos kept on attacking Greek cities with his Spartan army, and the gods began to fear him. Zeus tricked him into giving his power to the Blade of Olympus — and then killed him. In the Underworld, the Titan Gaia offered to help him.

Kratos decided to find the Sisters of Fate, who could change the past. He refused to give up, defeated the Sisters and went back in time to face Zeus again.

Chapter III · Mount Olympus · 2010

God of War III

Objective Destroy Olympus ✓ Completed

The war on Olympus began. Kratos didn't hesitate to destroy anyone in his way, and with every god he killed, the world fell into chaos. He couldn't help seeing his daughter in Pandora, the girl who helped him reach Zeus.

After the final duel, Athena wanted to take the power of Hope, hidden inside Kratos since he opened Pandora's Box. Instead, he chose to give it to humanity: he stabbed himself with the Blade of Olympus and released Hope to the world. Then his body disappeared…

Interlude · The Norse lands

A New Life in the North

Objective Start again

Somehow Kratos survived. Years later, he appeared in Midgard, the world of the Norse gods. He wanted to forget his past and avoided talking about it.

There he fell in love with Faye, a mysterious warrior, and they had a son: Atreus. Kratos hid the Blades of Chaos under the floor of his house, hoping never to use them again.

Chapter IV · Midgard · 2018

God of War

Objective Scatter Faye's ashes from the highest peak ✓ Completed

After Faye's death, Kratos and Atreus started a long journey to honour her last wish. Baldur, a god who couldn't feel anything, wouldn't stop attacking them. Mimir, the smartest man alive, agreed to help them, and Freya, Baldur's mother, became their friend.

When Atreus discovered that he was a god, Kratos finally decided to tell him the truth about his past. In the end, Kratos killed Baldur to save Freya.

Chapter V · The Nine Realms · 2022

God of War Ragnarök

Objective Survive Ragnarök ✓ Completed

Three winters later, Odin and Thor threatened to start a war. Atreus couldn't stop searching for answers about Loki. Freya, who wanted to kill Kratos for her son's death, finally agreed to fight by his side. After Odin killed their friend Brok, Kratos decided to lead the Nine Realms into war.

Kratos refused to kill Thor, and together they defeated Odin. At the end, Kratos let Atreus go to find the last giants.

Codex · Grammar

Verb Patterns

Verbs followed by the gerund or the infinitive — used in this story.

Gerund

verb + -ing
  • He couldn't stand losing.Prologue
  • He kept on attacking Greek cities.Ch. II
  • He couldn't help seeing his daughter.Ch. III
  • He avoided talking about his past.Midgard
  • Baldur wouldn't stop attacking.Ch. IV
  • Atreus couldn't stop searching.Ch. V

Infinitive with to

verb + to + infinitive
  • He promised to serve Ares.Prologue
  • He managed to destroy Ares.Ch. I
  • He refused to give up.Ch. II
  • He didn't hesitate to destroy anyone.Ch. III
  • Mimir agreed to help them.Ch. IV
  • Odin threatened to start a war.Ch. V

Infinitive without to

make / let / would rather + infinitive
  • Ares's trick made him kill his family.Prologue
  • He would rather die than live with it.Ch. I
  • The gods let him take Ares's place.Ch. I
  • Kratos let Atreus go.Ch. V

Colour code used in the story pages: orange = gerund · blue = infinitive with to · purple = infinitive without to.
